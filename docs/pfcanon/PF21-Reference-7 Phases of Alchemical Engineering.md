# 7 Phases of Alchemical Engineering

Use this 7‑phase alchemy map as a way to name *what mode you’re in* with a product: are you burning away old assumptions, opening options, narrowing, integrating, letting it live with users, refining, or solidifying? The power is in not mixing modes—once you know the phase, you can choose sane practices, expectations, and decision styles for that moment in the product’s life.

---

### **Hermetic Overlay (very compact)**

* **Ψ (Spirit)** – vision & meaning of the product (“why”).

* **Λ (Mind)** – models, architecture, strategy (“how”).

* **Σ (Body)** – code, processes, campaigns, org (“what”).

Each phase transforms all three, but below I’ll focus on concrete Σ‑level actions.

---

## **1\. Calcination – Burn down to the real problem**

**Definition**  
 Breaking down existing beliefs, plans, and assets until only the non‑negotiable essentials remain. It feels harsh: assumptions, vanity projects, and comforting illusions get “burned off.”

**Software dev / implementation**

* Audit existing code, systems, and tools; call out what’s brittle, obsolete, or blocking.

* Run spike prototypes purely to invalidate risky technical assumptions.

* Stop “feature accumulation”; ruthlessly kill half‑baked ideas and zombie branches.

**Business practices / operations**

* Pre‑mortems: “If this product failed in 18 months, what killed it?”

* Surface hard constraints (regulation, costs, talent, runway) instead of hiding them.

* Challenge org habits: meetings, approvals, and roles that don’t serve the new product.

**Marketing & growth**

* Listen for raw, unfiltered customer pain; treat criticism as fuel.

* Kill vague value props and buzzword decks; insist on one sharp problem statement.

* Drop channels that only serve vanity metrics (impressions, likes) with no learning.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Define the *core problem* and success criteria; decide what game you are refusing to play.

* Explicitly list “what this product will NOT do” to set boundaries.

* Decide whether this initiative survives at all; sometimes Calcination ends with a kill.

---

## **2\. Dissolution – Melt into possibilities**

**Definition**  
 Once the rigid shell is burned away, you “dissolve” what’s left into many options: open exploration, research, and ambiguity. Things are fluid on purpose.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Build quick, ugly prototypes to explore solutions, not to ship.

* Experiment with different tech stacks or architectures without committing.

* Pair design \+ engineering in low‑fidelity flows and clickable mockups.

**Business practices / operations**

* Keep the team structure light and flexible; minimal process, maximum exploration.

* Run discovery sessions across departments (sales, support, finance) to surface constraints.

* Try different ways of working (Kanban vs sprints, async vs sync) in “trial mode.”

**Marketing & growth**

* Conduct broad customer interviews and shadow sessions; collect language and stories.

* Test many rough positioning angles (landing pages, ads, pitch scripts) for signal.

* Explore multiple potential segments/use cases instead of picking one prematurely.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Map the problem space: adjacent jobs‑to‑be‑done, alternative solutions, substitutes.

* Explore multiple business models (usage‑based, seat, freemium, services‑plus‑product).

* Treat this as a divergent phase: postpone “final answer” decisions.

---

## **3\. Separation – Filter, sort, and choose**

**Definition**  
 You filter the fluid mass: separate signal from noise, core from peripheral, must‑have from nice‑to‑have. Options are winnowed.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Evaluate prototypes; choose the primary architecture and tech stack.

* Define clear system boundaries and interfaces (APIs, modules, services).

* Slice the backlog into *MVP vs later*; mark experiments as “keep,” “archive,” or “delete.”

**Business practices / operations**

* Decide core roles for this product (PM, tech lead, design, data, GTM owner).

* Choose a minimal set of processes (e.g., sprint reviews, incident handling) to formalize.

* Select a handful of key metrics (not 30\) that will drive decisions.

**Marketing & growth**

* Pick a primary persona and primary use case; stop trying to talk to everyone.

* Select 1–2 main acquisition channels to learn deeply.

* Drop weak messages that tested poorly; keep the one that actually moved people.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Define the MVP scope and acceptance criteria; commit to what “done for v1” means.

* Choose a go‑to‑market motion (product‑led, sales‑led, partner‑led) instead of blending all.

* Decide which future directions are deliberately *not* pursued this cycle.

---

## **4\. Conjunction – Combine into a working whole**

**Definition**  
 Previously separated elements—requirements, designs, architectures, and plans—are recombined into a coherent, functioning product. Integration becomes the main work.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Build the MVP end‑to‑end, integrating services, UI, and data flows.

* Establish CI/CD, shared environments, and integration tests.

* Resolve dependencies across teams; unify logging, monitoring, and error handling.

**Business practices / operations**

* Create a cross‑functional product squad (eng, design, PM, data, GTM).

* Align cadences: product planning, engineering sprints, and GTM timelines.

* Stand up operational basics: support inbox, on‑call rotation, incident playbooks.

**Marketing & growth**

* Align messaging across website, deck, docs, and sales scripts to tell one story.

* Coordinate launch readiness: pricing page, FAQs, onboarding emails, demo flows.

* Integrate feedback loops from sales/support back into the product team.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Articulate a single, cohesive product thesis (“who we serve, with what, and why now”).

* Align pricing, packaging, and feature tiers to that thesis.

* Plan the initial release train: v1, v1.1, and what happens in the first 90–180 days.

---

## **5\. Fermentation – Let the product live (and rot a bit)**

**Definition**  
 The product meets real users. Their use, abuse, and feedback introduce “new life” that changes the product. Some things spoil; new value cultures.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Ship to early adopters / beta and watch real usage patterns.

* Patch critical bugs quickly; prioritize stability over new features.

* Instrument the product (events, logs, traces) to observe behaviour, not just opinions.

**Business practices / operations**

* Stand up a basic customer support loop: ticketing, SLAs, escalation paths.

* Capture qualitative insights from support/success in a structured way.

* Start using operational data (churn, activation, support load) in planning.

**Marketing & growth**

* Run early‑access, beta, or design‑partner programs.

* Turn real user language into copy, case studies, and testimonials.

* Experiment with basic growth loops (invites, sharing, referrals) in lightweight ways.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Identify emergent behaviours and “weird” use cases that keep showing up.

* Decide if you’re seeing early product‑market fit or just noisy enthusiasm.

* Be willing to adjust positioning or roadmap based on what actually works, not what was planned.

---

## **6\. Distillation – Refine and systematize**

**Definition**  
 You repeatedly “boil off” what doesn’t matter and keep the essence: clean up the product, processes, and messaging using data and experience. This is where you get serious about quality and repeatability.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Refactor core code; pay down high‑interest tech debt.

* Harden performance, reliability, and security; add automated test coverage.

* Standardize patterns, libraries, and conventions across teams.

**Business practices / operations**

* Build regular cadences: OKRs, roadmap reviews, incident/post‑mortem reviews.

* Improve and automate recurring workflows (deploys, billing, renewals).

* Use dashboards/metrics to drive decisions, not just anecdotes.

**Marketing & growth**

* Double down on channels with proven CAC/LTV; prune the rest.

* Optimize funnel steps (activation, onboarding, expansion) with experimentation.

* Develop brand and style guidelines so assets feel consistent.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Define clear product‑market fit indicators and monitor them.

* Tighten focus on the core value loop—what users do that drives durable value.

* Codify “playbooks” for acquisition, onboarding, and expansion so others can run them.

---

## **7\. Coagulation – Solidify into a durable product & platform**

**Definition**  
 The refined essence “sets” into a stable form: a robust product, brand, and operating model. This becomes the new baseline—until the next cycle begins.

**Software dev / implementation**

* Establish predictable release trains and versioning (LTS, breaking‑change policy).

* Define platform boundaries and public APIs/SDKs for partners and integrations.

* Make conscious tradeoffs toward stability over rapid change in core surfaces.

**Business practices / operations**

* Formalize org structure, roles, and decision authorities around the product.

* Build governance for security, privacy, compliance, and risk.

* Lock in budgets, capacity planning, and hiring plans aligned with the product’s role.

**Marketing & growth**

* Clarify long‑term positioning and category narrative; aim for leadership, not just participation.

* Invest in durable assets: documentation, education, community, partnerships.

* Run larger‑scale campaigns and PR with a stable, recognizable brand.

**Product strategy & evolution**

* Decide which moats you are compounding (ecosystem, data, distribution, brand).

* Spin out new product lines or major bets on top of the “coagulated” platform.

* Start a *new* alchemical cycle for the next big initiative—burning down assumptions again at a higher level.

---

**Hermetic maxim:**  
 *As you refine the product, you refine the team; as above in vision, so below in code and practice.*
