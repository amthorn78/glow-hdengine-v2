## **Precedence, versioning, and scope**

### **1\. PF10 authority**

PF10 IS CANONICAL. For any topic explicitly addressed by an applicable, active PF10 addendum, that addendum is the current authoritative source of truth and supersedes conflicting permanent PF canon until the guidance is formally reviewed and drained into the relevant permanent PF document.

No competing canon may be used against an applicable PF10 addendum. Agents must not prefer, merge, reinterpret, or reconcile conflicting permanent-canon language while the PF10 guidance remains active.

PF10 authority is limited to the scope actually addressed by its addenda. The presence of a topic elsewhere in a PF10 document does not make PF10 authoritative over related topics that no addendum addresses.

### **2\. Scope within an individual PF10 document**

An individual PF10 document is a container for multiple numbered addenda. It must not be treated as one undifferentiated rule or as blanket authority over every topic associated with the current epic.

Each addendum is an independent unit of authority with its own scope. That scope is established by the addendum’s heading and substantive content, including the decisions, requirements, exceptions, artifacts, behaviors, processes, or canon topics it explicitly addresses.

Within one PF10 document:

1. Every active, non-superseded addendum remains authoritative for its own stated scope.  
2. A higher-numbered addendum does not automatically invalidate every lower-numbered addendum in the document.  
3. Unrelated addenda remain independently authoritative and must be applied together when more than one is relevant to the current task.  
4. An addendum must not be applied outside the scope established by its actual content.  
5. The entire document must be searched for relevant addenda. Agents must not consult only the first, last, or highest-numbered addendum.  
6. Document-level relevance does not establish addendum-level relevance. An agent must identify which specific addendum or addenda govern the current question.

For example, if addendum `2.12` governs evidence indexing and addendum `2.18` governs compatibility routing, both remain authoritative within their separate scopes. The existence of `2.18` does not supersede `2.12` merely because `2.18` has a higher number.

### **3\. Single-document and multi-document PF10 versions**

An active PF10 base version may consist of either:

1. One unlettered document, such as `10.x.x`; or  
2. One multi-document lettered set, such as `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, `10.x.xc`, and so forth.

A lettered set may be used when an epic’s build notes exceed the practical capacity of one document and drainage before the end of the epic is not practical.

All documents sharing the same base version and carrying sequential letter suffixes belong to the same PF10 version set. For example:

* `10.x.xa`  
* `10.x.xb`  
* `10.x.xc`

Together, these documents constitute the complete active `10.x.x` PF10 set.

The letter suffix identifies a physical document within the set. It does not create a newer base version, determine topical scope, or give one document greater authority than another.

All documents in the active lettered set are equally authoritative containers of PF10 addenda. A later lettered document extends the set. It does not replace, obsolete, drain, or supersede an earlier lettered document merely because it has a later suffix.

For example, `10.x.xb` does not replace `10.x.xa`. Active, non-superseded addenda in both documents remain authoritative for their respective scopes.

### **4\. Continuous addendum sequence**

A lettered PF10 set must preserve one continuous addendum-number sequence across all documents in the set.

Each subsequent lettered document must begin with the addendum number immediately following the final addendum in the preceding document.

Example:

* `10.x.xa` contains addenda through `2.28`.  
* `10.x.xb` begins with addendum `2.29`.  
* If `10.x.xb` ends with addendum `2.41`, `10.x.xc` begins with addendum `2.42`.

The numbered addendum sequence establishes the order of PF10 guidance. The document-letter sequence identifies where that guidance is stored.

### **5\. Individual-addendum supersession**

Supersession operates at the individual-addendum level, not automatically at the document level.

This rule applies:

* Between addenda in the same individual PF10 document.  
* Between addenda located in different documents within the same lettered PF10 set.

Each addendum remains authoritative for its stated scope unless a higher-numbered addendum revises, replaces, narrows, expands, or otherwise supersedes that scope.

When two or more addenda address the same or overlapping scope:

1. The highest-numbered applicable addendum is the sole authority for the overlapping scope.  
2. Conflicting or replaced language in every lower-numbered addendum is superseded and must not be applied in parallel.  
3. A lower-numbered addendum remains authoritative for any distinct scope not superseded by the higher-numbered addendum.  
4. If the higher-numbered addendum explicitly supersedes the earlier addendum in full, no portion of the earlier addendum remains authoritative.  
5. Supersession applies regardless of whether the addenda appear in the same document or in different documents within the active lettered set.

Example within one document:

* Addendum `2.17` establishes a rule.  
* Addendum `2.22` in the same document revises that rule.  
* Addendum `2.22` controls the revised scope.  
* Addendum `2.17` remains authoritative only for any distinct scope that `2.22` does not supersede.

Example across a lettered set:

* Addendum `2.17` appears in `10.x.xa`.  
* Addendum `2.34` appears in `10.x.xb` and revises the same rule.  
* Addendum `2.34` controls the overlapping scope.  
* Addendum `2.17` must not be used as competing or parallel authority for that scope.  
* Any distinct portion of addendum `2.17` not superseded by `2.34` remains authoritative.

A later document letter alone supersedes nothing. Addendum number and addendum scope determine supersession.

### **6\. Current-version rule**

Only the latest active PF10 base version matters. That version may be represented by one unlettered document or by a complete lettered set.

Older base versions are fully drained, obsolete, or both. Once a newer base version exists, agents must not read, reuse, compare, reconcile, or carry forward content from an older base version.

This rule does not apply between documents belonging to the same active lettered set. If `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, and `10.x.xc` form the active version, all three remain part of the authoritative PF10 source set.

An earlier lettered document remains authoritative for every applicable addendum it contains that has not been superseded by a higher-numbered addendum. It must not be discarded merely because a later lettered document exists.

### **7\. Live-content rule**

The active PF10 document or complete lettered set contains the current live guidance that has not yet been drained into its permanent PF home.

Drained guidance is removed when PF10 is formally revised.

If a lower-numbered addendum remains physically present after a higher-numbered addendum supersedes some or all of its scope, the superseded language is no longer live authority. Physical presence does not defeat individual-addendum supersession.

All unaffected, non-superseded addenda within the same document or lettered set remain live and authoritative for their respective scopes.

### **8\. Silence rule**

PF10 governs only through applicable, active, non-superseded addenda.

PF10 is silent when no addendum in the complete active PF10 document or lettered set addresses the current topic.

When PF10 is silent, authority returns to the relevant permanent PF-Canon document.

PF10 silence must be determined only after checking:

1. Every potentially relevant addendum within each applicable individual document.  
2. Every document belonging to the complete active lettered set, when a lettered set exists.  
3. Any higher-numbered addendum that may have superseded earlier guidance.

### **9\. Operational rule for agents**

Agents must apply PF10 in this order:

1. Identify the latest active PF10 base version.  
2. Determine whether it consists of one unlettered document or a multi-document lettered set.  
3. If it is a lettered set, identify all documents sharing that base version, such as `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, and `10.x.xc`.  
4. Treat each document as a container of separately scoped numbered addenda.  
5. Search every applicable document for all addenda relevant to the current topic.  
6. Determine the actual scope of each relevant addendum from its heading and substantive content.  
7. Compare the scopes of all relevant addenda, including addenda within the same document.  
8. Where addendum scopes overlap, apply only the highest-numbered applicable addendum to the overlapping scope.  
9. Continue applying lower-numbered addenda to any distinct scope not superseded by a higher-numbered addendum.  
10. Do not infer document-level supersession from a later letter suffix.  
11. Ignore all older PF10 base versions.  
12. Fall back to permanent PF canon only when the complete active PF10 document or lettered set contains no applicable, active, non-superseded guidance for the topic.

### Decision Summary

Replace the repeated PF09 governance and process preamble with one concise shared structure that preserves only PF09-owned task-register semantics and one phase-specific scope block in each phased PF09 document.

The shared PF09 preamble must retain:

All other still-operative material must be drained to its permanent owner and removed from the repeated PF09 preambles. Historical, superseded, or one-off material must not be copied into another permanent process document merely to preserve it.

This addendum assumes that mandatory acceptance tokens have already been retired, exact-source evidence is the current acceptance model, **PF30 \- HDE CRD Records** is canonical and available, and **PF06 \- Change Process Guide** governs both epics and CRDs.

### Origin and Evidence Basis

Origin: Product Owner direction to eliminate obsolete and duplicated governance material from the repeated PF09 preambles while preserving PF09-owned phase and task-register semantics.

Evidence basis:

* the common preamble currently repeated across the phased PF09 family;  
* the phase-specific scope distinctions already present in the individual PF09 documents;  
* the permanent single-home responsibilities established by the governing writing, governance, interface, change-process, schema, mechanics, QA, phase, reality-audit, plan-template, epic-record, and CRD-record documents; and

Sequence posture: This is an unnumbered standalone drain artifact. Assign its position in the active Build Notes addendum sequence only when it is formally inserted.

### Scope

This addendum governs consolidation of the common preamble repeated across PF09.1 through PF09.7. It does not change existing task bodies, task identifiers, task statuses, implementation requirements, or phase placement except where a separate authorized drain expressly does so.

Each phased PF09 document must retain its own phase-specific scope. A mechanically identical phase statement across all seven documents is non-conforming because the alchemical phase purpose, entry posture, in-scope transformation, and boundary differ by phase.

### Normative Consolidation Rules

1. **One local PF09 lane.** PF09 must state only the rules needed to determine what enters PF09, where it belongs, how it is identified, how its status is represented, and what supports completion.  
2. **No duplicated external canon.** PF09 must route governance, change process, schemas, mechanics, QA, phase theory, templates, and CRD records to their owning documents rather than reproducing those rules.  
3. **No token reintroduction.** PF09 must not require new acceptance tokens, complete token rosters, token claims, or token-to-evidence matrices. Existing token references remain historical compatibility metadata only.  
4. **No silent cross-ledger movement.** Epic-originated phased product work maps to PF09. CRD-originated work maps to PF30. Work moves or maps between those populations only through an explicit authorized decision.  
5. **No historical-policy resurrection.** Historical epic bindings, one-off close-pack requirements, temporary environment-variable rules, and old endpoint-proof instructions must remain historical unless current owning canon independently requires them.  
6. **No evidence-schema fork.** PF09 may name the evidence required by a task but must not carry a second copy of governed evidence schemas, field order, path catalogs, mirror rules, or validation procedures.  
7. **No QA-runbook fork.** PF09 may state required verification properties or harness-development work but must not embed full QA plans, QA execution procedures, or QA verdict rules.  
8. **Claim-state separation remains mandatory.** Repository presence, CI result, QA decision, acceptance, PF09 completion, epic or CRD closure, deployment, and production readiness remain separate claims governed by their owning sources.

## **2.2) Rename and Expand PF06 as the Change Process Guide**

**Sequence note:** Assign the next available addendum number when this entry is inserted into **HDE Build Notes**.

**Timestamp:** 082526 03:50

**Details:** Record the approved expansion of PF06 from an epic-only process guide into the common change-process guide governing both epics and CRDs.

### **Decision summary**

PF06 is no longer limited to the epic process.

Its canonical identity is now:

* **PF number:** PF06  
* **Canonical title:** Change Process Guide  
* **Canonical basename:** `PF06-Canon-Change-Process-Guide`

The applicable version suffix continues under the normal PF versioning process.

This is a rename and scope expansion of PF06, not the creation of a new PF document. The former **Epic Process Guide** title is superseded for active use.

### **Governing scope**

PF06 governs the common lifecycle for two change lanes:

1. **Epic lane**  
   * Used for product-development work originating in or formally mapped to PF09.  
   * Uses Epic identity and PF09 work-item accountability.  
2. **CRD lane**  
   * Used for observed features, defects, operational needs, governance needs, and system changes originating outside PF09.  
   * Uses CRD identity and PF30 accountability.  
   * May change canon only through an accompanying approved ADR.

The distinction between the lanes is their origin, identity, and controlling work register. It is not a difference in development, QA, evidence, or closure rigor.

### **Shared change lifecycle**

PF06 MUST define the common process used by both lanes:

1. Change intake or originating requirement  
2. Current-state and existing-work analysis  
3. Lead Developer plan  
4. Required approval  
5. Alchemical phase classification  
6. Implementation guidance and planning  
7. Phase-bounded PR and/or Ops execution  
8. Independent QA planning and execution  
9. Remediation and QA re-execution when required  
10. Exact-source evidence review  
11. Closeout decision  
12. Build Notes recording  
13. Canon drainage when applicable  
14. PF09 or PF30 record completion, according to the lane

Existing epic requirements remain operative unless PF06 explicitly changes them. The CRD lane reuses the established process by substituting CRD identity and PF30 work mapping where the epic lane uses Epic identity and PF09 mapping.

### **CRD terminology correction**

PF06 MUST NOT continue to use CRD solely as an intermediate document or subordinate stage inside every epic.

During drainage:

* CRD MUST be defined as the top-level change container for the PF30 lane.  
* Any legacy PF06 language treating CRD as a mandatory epic-internal artifact MUST be removed, relabeled, or otherwise resolved.  
* The replacement name or treatment of any former intermediate artifact may be settled during PF06 drainage, but it MUST NOT preserve two incompatible meanings for CRD.  
* Epic remains the top-level container for the PF09 lane.  
* CRD remains the top-level container for the PF30 lane.

### **Assurance parity**

The CRD lane MUST use the same applicable controls as the epic lane, including:

* approval boundaries;  
* alchemical phase separation;  
* PR and Ops discipline;  
* full QA under the **Glow QA Guide**;  
* exact candidate identification;  
* remediation and QA re-execution;  
* exact-source implementation and acceptance evidence;  
* separate implementation, QA, acceptance, and closure claims;  
* Build Notes recording; and  
* authorized closeout.

CRD does not mean abbreviated process, informal implementation, reduced QA, or automatic permission to change canon.

### **Document relationship**

PF06 owns the end-to-end change process and routes to the appropriate specialized authority:

* **HDE Governance** controls authority, permissions, approvals, and canon-change requirements.  
* **Plan Templates** controls reusable planning, implementation, QA, review, Ops, and closeout templates.  
* **Glow QA Guide** controls QA execution and acceptance.  
* **HDE Schemas and Artifacts** controls governed evidence, manifests, artifact identity, and path contracts.  
* **7 Phases of Alchemical Engineering** controls phase meaning and non-mixing.  
* **HDE Build Checklist** controls PF09 product-development work.  
* **PF30 CRD Register** controls CRD work registration and outcome tracking.  
* **HDE Build Notes** records material decisions, activity, results, and temporary pre-drain authority.

PF06 coordinates these authorities but does not replace them.

## **2.3) Establish the PF30-Tracked CRD Process**

**Timestamp:** 082526 02:57

**Details:** Establish a concise CRD process for observed features, defects, operational needs, and system changes that do not originate as PF09 product-development work.

### **Decision summary**

A CRD is a governed work container comparable to an epic. It may contain one or more PRs, Ops tasks, or both.

The principal differences are:

* A CRD begins with an observed change need rather than a PF09 task.  
* The Lead Developer analyzes that need and produces the CRD Plan.  
* CRD work is tracked in **PF30**, not PF09.  
* The downstream development, QA, remediation, evidence, review, and closeout process remains materially the same as the epic process.  
* A CRD may change canon only through an accompanying approved ADR.

### **Activation dependency**

This process MUST be drained only after the ADR retiring acceptance tokens and adopting exact-source evidence has been drained.

CRDs MUST use the resulting commit-, PR-, SHA-, workflow-, artifact-, and decision-based evidence model. This addendum does not reintroduce token rosters, acceptance-token matrices, or a parallel evidence system.

### **PF30 document model**

1. **PF30.1, PF30.2, and later PF30.x documents are rolling CRD-register volumes.**  
2. Each PF30.x volume MUST contain as many concise CRD records as it can practically support.  
3. A new PF30.x volume MUST NOT be created for each CRD.  
4. The active volume remains open until the Product Owner determines that its practical digital-processing limit is approaching.  
5. Only then may the next sequential PF30.x volume be opened.  
6. A CRD identifier is independent of the PF30.x volume containing it.  
7. A CRD SHOULD remain within one volume. If rollover makes that impractical, the two entries MUST contain reciprocal continuation pointers.  
8. Prior volumes remain authoritative historical records and MUST NOT be rewritten merely because a new volume is opened.

### **CRD process flow**

#### **1\. Intake**

A CRD begins with a concise description of an observed behavior, defect, feature need, infrastructure change, governance change, or other required system change.

The intake MUST identify:

* the observed condition or requested capability;  
* why change is warranted;  
* the affected system or governance surface;  
* known constraints or risks; and  
* available evidence.

The intake does not authorize implementation.

#### **2\. Lead Developer analysis**

The Lead Developer MUST inspect current repository and governing-document reality and determine:

* whether the request is valid and not already satisfied;  
* whether it belongs in the CRD lane;  
* its proposed scope and boundaries;  
* affected code, data, operations, documentation, and canon;  
* required PR and Ops work;  
* applicable alchemical phase or phase blocks;  
* dependencies and ordering;  
* QA and evidence requirements; and  
* whether an ADR is required.

The result becomes the CRD Plan.

#### **3\. PF30 registration and approval**

The CRD MUST receive a stable CRD ID and a concise initial record in the active PF30.x volume.

Implementation may begin only after the CRD Plan receives the approval required by current governance.

#### **4\. Alchemical phase separation**

Every planned PR or Ops task MUST belong to exactly one alchemical phase.

A CRD:

* MAY contain one phase;  
* MAY contain multiple ordered phase blocks;  
* MUST NOT be forced through all seven phases; and  
* MUST NOT mix different phase modes within one PR or Ops task.

Where multiple phase blocks exist, their dependencies and completion boundaries MUST be explicit. Completion of one phase does not imply completion of the entire CRD.

#### **5\. Implementation planning and execution**

After approval, the CRD follows the existing epic development process using CRD identity:

* implementation guidance and planning;  
* one or more phase-bounded PRs and/or Ops tasks;  
* review and correction;  
* exact-source implementation evidence; and  
* controlled candidate handoff to QA.

Existing epic templates SHOULD be reused by substituting:

* **CRD** for **Epic**; and  
* **PF30 work mapping** for **PF09 work-item mapping**.

No duplicate CRD-specific template system should be created where substitution preserves the required contract.

#### **6\. Canon changes**

A CRD MAY change canon, but it MUST have an accompanying approved ADR whenever it:

* changes a canonical rule, contract, schema, process, or interpretation;  
* supersedes current canon;  
* establishes a new permanent policy; or  
* changes the meaning of an existing governed artifact.

The ADR MUST identify the affected canon and drainage consequences. CRD activity and decisions MUST first be recorded in **HDE Build Notes** and then drained to permanent canon under the same authority and traceability rules used for epics.

A CRD Plan alone does not authorize canon supersession.

#### **7\. QA**

Every CRD requiring validation MUST undergo the full applicable process defined by the **Glow QA Guide**.

CRD QA MUST include, as applicable:

* a separate QA Plan;  
* an exact candidate identity;  
* required automated and manual checks;  
* evidence collection;  
* PASS, FAIL, or blocked disposition;  
* remediation and re-execution after material candidate changes; and  
* a final QA verdict bound to the accepted candidate.

CI success alone is not QA acceptance. CRD QA MUST NOT be replaced by a lightweight check inserted into development.

#### **8\. Closeout**

A CRD may close only when its applicable implementation, Ops, QA, evidence, ADR, and drainage obligations have received the required decisions.

Closeout MUST distinguish:

* work implemented;  
* QA accepted;  
* Ops completed;  
* canon drained or explicitly deferred;  
* residual or deferred work;  
* superseded or rejected scope; and  
* the authorized closure decision.

#### **9\. PF30 result update**

At closure, the existing PF30 CRD record MUST be updated with the concise final result.

PF30 is the CRD register—not the implementation log, QA archive, or evidence store. Detailed activity remains in its governed source and is referenced from PF30.

### **Minimum PF30 CRD record**

#### **Initial record**

* CRD ID and title  
* Status  
* Creation date  
* Observed need or requested change  
* Lead Developer disposition and bounded scope  
* Alchemical phase block or blocks  
* Planned PR and Ops units with dependencies  
* Canon impact and accompanying ADR reference, when applicable  
* QA requirement  
* CRD Plan and material Build Notes references

#### **Closure update**

* Final status and closure date  
* Implemented PR, commit, merge, and Ops references  
* QA verdict and exact candidate identity  
* Material deviations or deferred work  
* ADR and canon-drain status  
* Final authorized outcome

PF30 SHOULD NOT reproduce full plans, commands, logs, evidence payloads, QA runbooks, or retrospectives. Intermediate PF30 updates are required only when a material scope, status, or authority change would otherwise make the record misleading.
