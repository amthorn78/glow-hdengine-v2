## **Precedence, versioning, and scope**

### **1\. PF10 authority**

PF10 IS CANONICAL. For any topic explicitly addressed by an applicable, active PF10 addendum, that addendum is the current authoritative source of truth and supersedes conflicting permanent PF canon until the guidance is formally reviewed and drained into the relevant permanent PF document.

No competing canon may be used against an applicable PF10 addendum. Agents must not prefer, merge, reinterpret, or reconcile conflicting permanent-canon language while the PF10 guidance remains active.

PF10 authority is limited to the scope actually addressed by its addenda. The presence of a topic elsewhere in a PF10 document does not make PF10 authoritative over related topics that no addendum addresses.

### **2\. Scope within an individual PF10 document**

An individual PF10 document is a container for multiple numbered addenda. It must not be treated as one undifferentiated rule or as blanket authority over every topic associated with the current epic.

Each addendum is an independent unit of authority with its own scope. That scope is established by the addendum’s heading and substantive content, including the decisions, requirements, exceptions, artifacts, behaviors, processes, or canon topics it explicitly addresses.

Within one PF10 document:

1. Every active, non-superseded addendum remains authoritative for its own stated scope.  
2. A higher-numbered addendum does not automatically invalidate every lower-numbered addendum in the document.  
3. Unrelated addenda remain independently authoritative and must be applied together when more than one is relevant to the current task.  
4. An addendum must not be applied outside the scope established by its actual content.  
5. The entire document must be searched for relevant addenda. Agents must not consult only the first, last, or highest-numbered addendum.  
6. Document-level relevance does not establish addendum-level relevance. An agent must identify which specific addendum or addenda govern the current question.

For example, if addendum `2.12` governs evidence indexing and addendum `2.18` governs compatibility routing, both remain authoritative within their separate scopes. The existence of `2.18` does not supersede `2.12` merely because `2.18` has a higher number.

### **3\. Single-document and multi-document PF10 versions**

An active PF10 base version may consist of either:

1. One unlettered document, such as `10.x.x`; or  
2. One multi-document lettered set, such as `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, `10.x.xc`, and so forth.

A lettered set may be used when an epic’s build notes exceed the practical capacity of one document and drainage before the end of the epic is not practical.

All documents sharing the same base version and carrying sequential letter suffixes belong to the same PF10 version set. For example:

* `10.x.xa`  
* `10.x.xb`  
* `10.x.xc`

Together, these documents constitute the complete active `10.x.x` PF10 set.

The letter suffix identifies a physical document within the set. It does not create a newer base version, determine topical scope, or give one document greater authority than another.

All documents in the active lettered set are equally authoritative containers of PF10 addenda. A later lettered document extends the set. It does not replace, obsolete, drain, or supersede an earlier lettered document merely because it has a later suffix.

For example, `10.x.xb` does not replace `10.x.xa`. Active, non-superseded addenda in both documents remain authoritative for their respective scopes.

### **4\. Continuous addendum sequence**

A lettered PF10 set must preserve one continuous addendum-number sequence across all documents in the set.

Each subsequent lettered document must begin with the addendum number immediately following the final addendum in the preceding document.

Example:

* `10.x.xa` contains addenda through `2.28`.  
* `10.x.xb` begins with addendum `2.29`.  
* If `10.x.xb` ends with addendum `2.41`, `10.x.xc` begins with addendum `2.42`.

The numbered addendum sequence establishes the order of PF10 guidance. The document-letter sequence identifies where that guidance is stored.

### **5\. Individual-addendum supersession**

Supersession operates at the individual-addendum level, not automatically at the document level.

This rule applies:

* Between addenda in the same individual PF10 document.  
* Between addenda located in different documents within the same lettered PF10 set.

Each addendum remains authoritative for its stated scope unless a higher-numbered addendum revises, replaces, narrows, expands, or otherwise supersedes that scope.

When two or more addenda address the same or overlapping scope:

1. The highest-numbered applicable addendum is the sole authority for the overlapping scope.  
2. Conflicting or replaced language in every lower-numbered addendum is superseded and must not be applied in parallel.  
3. A lower-numbered addendum remains authoritative for any distinct scope not superseded by the higher-numbered addendum.  
4. If the higher-numbered addendum explicitly supersedes the earlier addendum in full, no portion of the earlier addendum remains authoritative.  
5. Supersession applies regardless of whether the addenda appear in the same document or in different documents within the active lettered set.

Example within one document:

* Addendum `2.17` establishes a rule.  
* Addendum `2.22` in the same document revises that rule.  
* Addendum `2.22` controls the revised scope.  
* Addendum `2.17` remains authoritative only for any distinct scope that `2.22` does not supersede.

Example across a lettered set:

* Addendum `2.17` appears in `10.x.xa`.  
* Addendum `2.34` appears in `10.x.xb` and revises the same rule.  
* Addendum `2.34` controls the overlapping scope.  
* Addendum `2.17` must not be used as competing or parallel authority for that scope.  
* Any distinct portion of addendum `2.17` not superseded by `2.34` remains authoritative.

A later document letter alone supersedes nothing. Addendum number and addendum scope determine supersession.

### **6\. Current-version rule**

Only the latest active PF10 base version matters. That version may be represented by one unlettered document or by a complete lettered set.

Older base versions are fully drained, obsolete, or both. Once a newer base version exists, agents must not read, reuse, compare, reconcile, or carry forward content from an older base version.

This rule does not apply between documents belonging to the same active lettered set. If `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, and `10.x.xc` form the active version, all three remain part of the authoritative PF10 source set.

An earlier lettered document remains authoritative for every applicable addendum it contains that has not been superseded by a higher-numbered addendum. It must not be discarded merely because a later lettered document exists.

### **7\. Live-content rule**

The active PF10 document or complete lettered set contains the current live guidance that has not yet been drained into its permanent PF home.

Drained guidance is removed when PF10 is formally revised.

If a lower-numbered addendum remains physically present after a higher-numbered addendum supersedes some or all of its scope, the superseded language is no longer live authority. Physical presence does not defeat individual-addendum supersession.

All unaffected, non-superseded addenda within the same document or lettered set remain live and authoritative for their respective scopes.

### **8\. Silence rule**

PF10 governs only through applicable, active, non-superseded addenda.

PF10 is silent when no addendum in the complete active PF10 document or lettered set addresses the current topic.

When PF10 is silent, authority returns to the relevant permanent PF-Canon document.

PF10 silence must be determined only after checking:

1. Every potentially relevant addendum within each applicable individual document.  
2. Every document belonging to the complete active lettered set, when a lettered set exists.  
3. Any higher-numbered addendum that may have superseded earlier guidance.

### **9\. Operational rule for agents**

Agents must apply PF10 in this order:

1. Identify the latest active PF10 base version.  
2. Determine whether it consists of one unlettered document or a multi-document lettered set.  
3. If it is a lettered set, identify all documents sharing that base version, such as `10.x.xa`, `10.x.xb`, and `10.x.xc`.  
4. Treat each document as a container of separately scoped numbered addenda.  
5. Search every applicable document for all addenda relevant to the current topic.  
6. Determine the actual scope of each relevant addendum from its heading and substantive content.  
7. Compare the scopes of all relevant addenda, including addenda within the same document.  
8. Where addendum scopes overlap, apply only the highest-numbered applicable addendum to the overlapping scope.  
9. Continue applying lower-numbered addenda to any distinct scope not superseded by a higher-numbered addendum.  
10. Do not infer document-level supersession from a later letter suffix.  
11. Ignore all older PF10 base versions.  
12. Fall back to permanent PF canon only when the complete active PF10 document or lettered set contains no applicable, active, non-superseded guidance for the topic.

## 2.3 Codex security review is deprecated: it is no longer a step of the HDE pull-request review chain

Timestamp: 101326 02:55 (UTC). Details: records the Product Owner's deprecation of the automated Codex security review, which is no longer available. Origin: the HDE-CRD-0002 WU-PR-01 PR-35 session for pull request [amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2\#627](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/627). Evidence: the Codex review summary of [\#627](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/627) and the precedent of [\#501](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/issues/501) (below).

### Source and authority

This addendum records the Product Owner's directions of 2026-10-13, given in the HDE-CRD-0002 WU-PR-01 PR-35 session:

* "This feature (security review) is no longer available. We will need to deprecate it. You may create a PF10 build notes addendum to specify its deprecation."  
* Before that direction, the Product Owner's decision for [\#627](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/627) was "Waive it for [\#627](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/627)", given in answer to the question of how PR-35 should treat the security review that the HDE-CRD-0002 Implementation Plan names in its reviewer chain and that never ran on [\#627](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/627).

Where this addendum conflicts with earlier PF-Canon or with an in-flight change document, this addendum governs for the scope below.

### Rule

1. Scope. The automated Codex security review of a pull request: the "Security Review" row of the Codex review summary, produced when a pull request opens or requested by an @codex security review comment. It is covered wherever HDE canon, an in-flight change document, a GCFPE prompt or a skill names it as part of a pull request's review chain.  
2. Deprecation. The Codex security review is not part of the HDE pull-request review chain. It is not obtained or waited on, and its absence is not treated as a review gap, finding, blocker or waiver need, by any of: a pull request, a work unit, a merge-readiness decision, a lineage review, a QA readiness decision or a closure decision. A MERGE\_PENDING result does not depend on it.  
3. Unchanged. The Codex code review remains part of the pull-request review chain and is read and resolved as before. Every other security obligation is unchanged, including:  
   * security constraints in permanent canon;  
   * security constraints in approved Specifications and Implementation Plans;  
   * security constraints in the repository's agent instructions;

## 2.11 Every requirement and work unit carries a business case; work rests on real inputs only

Timestamp: 102026 10:35 (UTC). Details: Establishes that no requirement or work unit is done without a stated business case or on inputs the product never has, and sets the rules for where the business case is checked, what counts as a real input, the ban on emulating external systems, how review findings are dispositioned, and when a repeated finding class stops the fixing of instances. Origin: the HDE-CRD-0002 WU-PR-06 postmortem v1.0 (HDE-CRD-0002-WU-PR-06-postmortem-v1.0.md, INCIDENT\_RCA, analysis state COMPLETE, author session https\://claude.ai/code/session\_01NZEtMVb5HR3XYEmJ6W5cb6), written at the Product Owner's request in the WU-PR-06 PR-20/PR-30 session before PR-40. ### Source and authority

This addendum records the Product Owner's rules, in the Product Owner's words: "Every PR must have a business case. It cannot simply be for the sake of evidence or CI." and "no work is ever done based on inputs the product will never have." The Product Owner directed that entry into HDE Build Notes is adoption (direction of 2026-10-20 in the authoring conversation). Where this addendum conflicts with earlier PF-Canon or with an in-flight change document, this addendum governs for the scope below.

### Rule

1. Business case. Every Specification requirement (Epic or CRD) and every work unit states:  
   * its beneficiary: who gains (Glow users, the production operator, or the delivery team);  
   * its evidence: the observed incidents or measurements that show the problem, cited; a forecast is not evidence;  
   * its benefit: what changes for the beneficiary, in user outcome, risk, time or money;  
   * its measure: how the benefit is checked after delivery;  
   * its cost ceiling: the most the work may spend before it returns to the Product Owner, in sessions and elapsed days. This is a business-case budget and not a CI-budget control.  
2. More complete or more faithful evidence, CI, tooling or records for their own sake, conformance to canon, and "could prevent" without an observed incident are not a business case. Internal tooling qualifies only when its expected saving, from observed incident data, exceeds its ceiling.  
3. Gates.  
   * Specification approval blocks a requirement without a business case, for CRDs and Epics alike.  
   * IA-30 checks each work unit's business case and ceiling.  
   * PR-10 carries both into the instruction, and PR-20 states how the plan stays within the ceiling.  
   * The Proceed is given against them.  
   * A unit whose business case is met stops; further work on it needs a new business case.  
   * A unit that passes its ceiling has a change of "budget, schedule or risk needing Product Owner direction", which is material under the existing definition, and the work returns to the Product Owner before it continues.  
4. Real inputs. Requirements, designs, tests, fixes and review dispositions rest on real inputs. An input is real when it:  
   * (a) exists on main, or in an approved unit's planned deliverable;  
   * (b) occurs in the product's real operating environment: the deployed services, the hosted CI runner, the operator's shell, the vendor's responses; or  
   * (c) can be supplied by a user, a vendor or an attacker through an interface the product exposes.  
5. An input constructed only to exercise code justifies no requirement, design element, test, fix or blocking finding. An input outside the real set gets one refusal or a stated limit, never a model of it.  
6. No emulation of external systems. A tool or test does not reimplement the behavior of an external system: GitHub Actions, pip and Python packaging, YAML, a vendor API, PostgreSQL. It pins the exact input it supports and refuses any other, or it calls the real system.  
7. Finding disposition.  
   * A review finding, from Codex or from a person, is fixed when it shows a wrong result, a crash or a guarantee breach on a real input (rule 3).  
   * Otherwise the reply on the thread gives the traced path and the stated limit, and the thread is resolved.  
   * Declining a P1 or a security finding goes to the Product Owner first.  
   * A blanket instruction to fix every finding on a PR is read under this rule.  
   * This makes the dispositions PO-35-5 and PO-35-6 of the WU-PR-06 pull request the standing default: fix only a wrong result on a real input, or a breach of the read-only or no-secrets guarantees; a snapshot-precision finding is a hypothetical-input finding unless it shows a write that the tool itself, or code it runs in the checkout, can make.  
8. Repeated finding class. When two consecutive review rounds on one PR raise findings of the same class (the same component or contract), the session stops fixing instances and asks the Product Owner one design question: narrow the design, accept a stated limit, or rescope. This is a design trigger by finding class. It is not a push count, a pause threshold or a CI-budget control, and it leaves the Change Process Guide §0.2 rule "There is no five-update circuit breaker" unchanged.

## 2.14 A P1 finding is addressed until it is resolved; only a finding that depends on an input the product will never have may be declined

Timestamp: 102026 18:55 (UTC). Details: Records the Product Owner's rule that no P1 review finding is declined, deferred, or accepted as a stated limit while its triggering input is real; the one exception is a finding whose triggering input the product will never have. Origin: the Product Owner's direction of 2026-10-20 in the continuing HDE-CRD-0002 Implementation Agent session, after the WU-PR-07 PR-35 session offered to decline two P1 Codex findings (CX7-2 and CX7-3 on pull request [amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2\#668](https://github.com/amthorn78/glow-hdengine-v2/pull/668)) as stated limits. Evidence: the PR-35 session's decision request and the two findings, both checked against the code on the pull request's head and against Change Process Guide §3.5.2.1, which requires the behaviour each finding reports missing.

### Source and authority

This addendum records the Product Owner's rule, in the Product Owner's words: "I do NOT want to ignore P1 findings ever. If we have some rule to do that now, it is wrong" and "P1s need to be addressed until the end, unless the violate the rule where they are testing for inputs that will never exist in this app." The Product Owner directed that this be recorded as an HDE Build Notes addendum. Where this addendum conflicts with earlier PF-Canon, an earlier addendum or an in-flight change document, this addendum governs for the scope below.

### Rule

1. Scope. A P1 finding is any review finding labelled P1, or any higher priority such as P0, by the Codex code review or by a person, on any pull request of any Epic or CRD.  
2. Addressed until resolved. A P1 finding whose triggering input is real is fixed. It is not declined, deferred to a later change, recorded as a stated limit, or resolved on cost, effort, design preference, a plan's earlier choice, an ADR's stated limit, or a "not worth its code" judgement.  
   * Fixed means the wrong result, crash or guarantee breach the finding reports no longer occurs on that real input. A test shows it, and the test fails without the fix.  
   * A typed refusal of the input is a fix when refusing it is correct behaviour for the product.  
   * The thread stays open until the fix is pushed and the finding's own check of it is answered. A pull request with an open P1 thread on a real input is not merge-ready.  
3. The one exception. A P1 finding may be declined only when its triggering input is not real under addendum 2.11 rule 4: it does not exist on main or in an approved unit's planned deliverable, does not occur in the product's real operating environment, and cannot be supplied by a user, vendor or attacker through an interface the product exposes. The input is then one constructed only to exercise the code (addendum 2.11 rule 5).  
   * The reply on the thread gives the traced path and shows why the input can never occur in the product.  
   * As before, declining a P1 goes to the Product Owner first, and the thread is resolved only after that.  
4. Repeated finding class. Addendum 2.11 rule 8 still applies: two consecutive rounds raising findings of the same class stop the fixing of instances, and the session asks the Product Owner one design question. For a P1 on a real input, "accept a stated limit" is not an option. ### The close-run evidence family (introduced)

HDE Schemas & Artifacts §8.17 requires a new recurring QA evidence family to be introduced through a Build Notes addendum. This addendum introduces one:

* Path. audit/qa/\<change-dir\>/close/\<check\_id\>/primary.log, each with its sibling .path\_proof.txt, for a CRD admitted in the canonical updater's CRD admission table. \<change-dir\> is the lower-case directory form, for example hde-crd-0002.  
* What it holds. Same-run mechanical evidence of a change's close workflow: one primary per close-run check, rendered from that check's captured command, exit code and output as a PF27 v2 step-log header. Its posture is PASS, FAIL or BLOCKED, as Glow QA Guide "Close-pack truthfulness and same-run execution" requires.  
* Status. A post-verdict family. It is written after the final QA verdict, and it neither reopens nor adds to the change's approved qa\_step\_logs\_manifest.json or any checks/ primary.  
* Writer. Only the canonical updater, tools/evidence/update\_evidence\_index.py, writes it, through its close-run publication entrypoint inside one recoverable transaction. The given results replace the whole family. The entrypoint requires the change's recorded QA family to exist.  
* Membership. The family's members are the validated primaries and path proofs under close/, in a closed shape; anything else under close/ is refused.  
  * This is a scoped exception, for the close/ directory only, to §8.17 "Current-state evidence": "Tools MUST NOT infer run state by enumerating subdirectories". That rule continues to govern checks/ and every other part of a QA root, where qa\_step\_logs\_manifest.json stays the authoritative index.
