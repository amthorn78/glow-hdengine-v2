## Literal and placement conventions

- Each literal is the exact bytes between the LF that ends its opening fence line and the LF that begins its closing fence line. Fences are four backticks; no literal contains more than one consecutive backtick.
- A `REPLACE` old block is a run of complete original lines joined by LF, without the final line's LF. That LF lies outside the span and is not changed. The replacement block is written the same way.
- An `INSERT` gap is defined by two literal anchors that are contiguous in the original: the BEFORE anchor, its terminating LF, then the AFTER anchor. The inserted bytes are the literal followed by exactly one LF. Neither anchor changes.
- Trailing spaces are part of the literals. Where a literal line ends in two spaces, the fence keeps them; the byte counts and SHA-256 values below (UTF-8, no added LF) let the applier confirm exact bytes.
- Line numbers and byte offsets refer to the unchanged original. Offsets are zero-based, the end offset exclusive.
